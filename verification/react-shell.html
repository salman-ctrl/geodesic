Explore the maths

Newton's gravitational force F = GMm / r². Dividing by spacecraft mass m gives gravitational acceleration:

a = GM / r² = μ / r²

For a circular orbit the speed is v = √(GM / r). For other speeds, the Kepler propagator calculates an elliptical or escaping path. Below the surface, the craft impacts the planet.

03 · The core idea

A gravity assist is borrowed speed.

A spacecraft falls toward a moving planet, swings behind it and leaves in a new direction. Seen from the planet, it leaves exactly as fast as it arrived.

Seen from the Sun, it has taken a little of the planet's orbital speed with it. No propellant burned. The planet slows by an amount too small to ever measure.

05 · The real science, in plain language

Three methods power the flight engine.

Δv = Isp · g₀ · ln(m₀ / m₁)

The rocket equation

How much speed your propellant can buy. The catch is the logarithm: every extra kilogram on top needs many more underneath. This is why rockets drop stages, and why saving speed with a flyby matters so much.

v² = μ (2/r − 1/a)

Vis-viva

How fast you travel at any point of any orbit. It prices every burn in the game: leaving Earth, braking at Mars, and why burning deep in a gravity well gives more for less.

r₁, r₂, Δt → orbit

Patched conics + Lambert

Near a planet, only that planet pulls. Far away, only the Sun. Stitch the pieces and you have a trajectory. Lambert's problem finds the arc that joins two planets in a given travel time.
